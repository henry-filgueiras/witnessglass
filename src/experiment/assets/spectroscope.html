Haar compares neighbouring halves of progressively larger time windows. Strong detail means the two halves differ. A repeating figure and a long block therefore leave different signatures across scales — a figure’s contrast collapses once a half-window reaches its period, while a block’s peaks at the scale straddling its edge. None of this is a frequency.

Window

A Matrix Profile slides a window of fixed length and records, for each position, the distance to its most similar non-overlapping neighbour. Low means “something else here looks like this”. Distances are normalized per window, so they are blind to amplitude: the same shape at a different intensity is a perfect match.

A display choice, not the recording. The canonical recording is a list of records with timestamps; every raster on this page is one way of counting them into buckets. No transform here derived an optimum width, and Haar in particular operates on whatever sampling it is handed.
